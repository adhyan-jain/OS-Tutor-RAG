## A. Exact surviving research question

> When an LLM judges whether an execution trace of a CPU-scheduling or synchronisation problem is valid, with the rules (including tie and interleaving nondeterminism) stated in the prompt, does supplying one **valid** reference trace make the judge reject a **different valid** trace — beyond the effect of adding any context (no reference, irrelevant reference, reworded reference)?

The question is about **LLM judges with references**, on **small open-weight models (7–9B, one per family)**, in **two OS domains with exact validity oracles**. It is *not* about program reasoning in general, LLMs in general, or educational grading in practice.

## B. Strongest prior-art threat

**Yeadon et al. 2026** ([arXiv 2603.14732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14732)) and ***Judging Against the Reference*** ([arXiv 2601.07506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07506)). Together with Kranti & Vajjala 2026 ([arXiv 2607.12885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12885)), they already show that LLM judges defer to supplied references, using false references, swapped references, and the presence or absence of a reference. The "RSG exists" idea is the founding motivation of functional-correctness code evaluation (Kulal 2019; Chen et al. 2021, [arXiv 2107.03374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374)). It is **killed as a contribution**.

What survives, as far as this targeted search found:
- a **valid-but-different** reference, rather than a false one;
- a domain with an **exact oracle** and **controllable |V(P)|**;
- **context controls** that separate anchoring from generic context effects.

This is a measurement contribution, not a new phenomenon. Full analysis: `docs/PRIOR_ART_FINAL.md`.

**Label checks**, all passing (`pytest tests/research`, 21 tests):
- **Two independent oracles.** The enumerators (`cpu_scheduler.py`, `concurrency_interleaver.py`: successor relation, memoised counts, membership) and the rule validators (`validators.py`, which import neither) agree on every enumerated trace, every mutant, and thousands of random candidates. That covers ~195k cross-checks during development, plus the seeded test suite.
- **Every stored label is re-verified:**
  - V(P,R1) = V(P,R2) = V(P,R3) = true, and V(P,I) = V(P,I2) = false.
  - R1 equals the canonical trace.
  - \|V(P)\| matches the exact count.
  - Where \|V(P)\| ≤ 20,000, full enumeration confirms membership as well.
- **Textbook cases are pinned:**
  - Silberschatz FCFS: \|V\| = 6 for three simultaneous arrivals.
  - SJF: unique schedule.
  - RR q=4: the textbook trace is accepted.
  - The RR arrival-vs-requeue ambiguity admits both orders.
  - The mutex is non-re-entrant.
  - A semaphore wait on zero is rejected.
- **"Alternative" is not superficial.** In scheduling, R2 has a different waiting-time vector from R1 in every instance. In concurrency, R2 lies in a different Mazurkiewicz class (the per-object order differs), so it is not a commuting reshuffle. It is not a renaming or formatting change, and not a random permutation (R2 is sampled uniformly from V(P) \ {R1}).
- **Hand spot-checks:** 8 sampled instances (id_030, id_081, rr_primitive_064/065, struct_mutex_013/028, struct_semaphore_076/079) were worked through by hand against the stated rules. All labels are correct.
- **No duplicate problems** anywhere, including against the auxiliary filtering set.

**Benchmark limitations:**
- **Scheduling invalid-trace mix.** After leakage filtering, scheduling invalid traces are dominated by one violation type, "process runs before it arrives" (id: 132/150). Policy-violation mutants are a minority, so judge specificity is measured mostly on one error type.
- **Selection effect.** The filters reject many candidate problems, which biases the benchmark toward problems that admit a locally plausible invalid trace.
- **Construct.** Prompts state that ties are free. Real courses often fix a tie-break convention (§L).

## D. Leakage status

Thresholds were frozen in `docs/PREREGISTRATION_V2.md` §3 before the audit: an attacker fails if the lower bound of its 95% CI is above chance + 0.05. They were never moved.

- **First v2 audit: FAILED.** Divergence position 0.591 [0.570, 0.613], reference similarity 0.608 [0.586, 0.631], local plausibility 0.602 [0.579, 0.625].
  - **Mechanism:** valid traces share their opening with R1 more often, and invalid concurrency traces contained adjacent pairs never seen in valid traces.
  - **This is the result AGY's audit would have hidden.**
- **Redesign (deviation D2, recorded in the prereg before any model call):**
  - Every adjacent pair of I must occur in some valid trace.
  - I is matched exactly to R2 on length, first and last divergence from R1, and edit distance to R1.
  - Adversarial filtering uses attackers trained on a disjoint auxiliary benchmark (seed 99173).
- **Final audit:** `research/results/leakage_audit_v2.json`. Primary target R2 vs I; grouped 5-fold CV × 5 seeds.

Accuracy with 95% CI; the bar is a lower bound > 0.55.

**Status:**
- **Scheduling: PASS on every attacker.**
- **Concurrency: FAIL** on the adjacent-pair attacker (D2) and the embedding attacker (D3).
- **Pooled: FAIL**, on embeddings only, and that failure is driven by concurrency.

Iteration stopped there, because further filtering against the audit's own attackers would overfit it. Under D2/D3, fixed before any model output, **every valid-vs-invalid discrimination claim is restricted to the scheduling splits**. The anchoring tests (H2/H3) remain valid on all splits, because they compare the *same* R2 under different references and I does not enter them.

One note on the diagnostic: even a model given the problem text plus the trace stays at chance (0.51). The task cannot be learned shallowly from these 630 instances.

## E. Central experimental result (exact-match vs semantic scoring, E1)

**PENDING PHASE B.**
- **Framing (fixed in advance):** RSG = Acc_sem − Acc_ref ≥ 0 by construction. It is reported as magnitude with a CI and the canonical-choice rate, never as a finding.
- **Dry-run sanity:**
  - An oracle generator that samples uniformly from V(P) gets Acc_ref = 0.127 and Acc_sem = 1.000 (RSG 0.873).
  - A generator that always outputs R1 gets RSG = 0.

  This shows the gap measures *which* valid trace a model happens to emit, not its competence.

## F. Reference-sensitivity result (E2)

**PENDING PHASE B.** The preregistered tests:
- **H2:** the anchoring index AI = accept(R2 \| ref = R2) − accept(R2 \| ref = R1), exact one-sided McNemar.
- **H2b:** controls with no reference, an irrelevant reference, a reworded R1, and an invalid reference.
- **H3:** the R1↔R3 flip rate against the measured noise floor.
- **Multiplicity:** Holm correction across models.
- **Mitigation arm:** the note "other valid executions may exist".

The analysis code is verified on sanity baselines:
- **Exact-match judge:** AI = 1.00, H2 p = 2e-190. H2b correctly *fails*, because exact match rejects equally with no reference, so the effect is not attributed to the reference.
- **Oracle judge:** AI = 0.
- **Coin-flip judge:** AI = 0.006 [−0.049, 0.063].

## G. Model-ranking result (E3)

**PENDING PHASE B.**
- **Primary:** Kendall τ between Acc_ref and Acc_sem rankings, plus P(τ < 1) over 2,000 instance bootstraps.
- **Secondary:** rankings from one reference-guided LLM judge vs the oracle. The judge is chosen by a frozen rule.

With 5–6 models the test has little power. A "no change" result will be reported as such, and so will the fact that the prior (Yeadon et al.) is that rankings survive reference effects.

## H. Branching-scaling result (E4)

**PENDING PHASE B.**
- **Method:** logistic regression of accept(R2 \| R1) and of the E1 gap on log2\|V\|, trace length, number of entities, dist(R1,R2) and domain, with cluster-bootstrap CIs, plus quartile trends.
- **Pre-specified interpretation:** "systematic" requires the log2\|V\| coefficient's CI to exclude 0.
- **Caveat known in advance:** \|V\| is confounded with trace length and domain (concurrency has far larger \|V\|), so a raw correlation with \|V\| does not establish a branching effect.

## I. OOD result (E5)

**PENDING PHASE B.** AI and E1 metrics per split: lexical (odd names plus table format), long traces, high branching, a different primitive (RR), structural (mutex) and unseen shapes (semaphore). If AI's CI includes 0 on a split where id's excludes 0, the disappearance becomes a central limitation.

## J. Strongest defensible contributions (as of Phase A)

1. **A verified benchmark** for judging nondeterministic OS execution traces:
   - exact two-oracle labels;
   - substantive (non-Mazurkiewicz-equivalent) alternatives;
   - distance-, profile- and adjacency-matched invalid traces;
   - a published leakage audit, including the honest first failure, a clean scheduling pass, and residual concurrency failures.
2. **A controlled ablation design** that can separate reference anchoring from generic context effects in LLM judging: none, irrelevant, reworded, valid-other, self and invalid references, plus a mitigation note and a measured noise floor.
3. **(Conditional on Phase B)** the H2/H2b/H3 results, whichever way they come out. A clean null ("small judges are not anchored by valid alternative references beyond context effects") is publishable as a short or negative-results paper. A positive H2 that fails H2b is a "context sensitivity" note, not an anchoring paper.

## L. Remaining limitations

1. **Phase B has not run.** Every hypothesis-level conclusion is pending.
2. **Small, local models only:** 7–9B, quantised, temperature 0. No frontier or API models, and no reasoning mode (qwen3 runs with think=false). Results may not transfer to larger judges.
3. **Concurrency leakage.** It fails two gating attackers: adjacent pairs 0.628 and bge embeddings 0.710. That failure also pulls the pooled embedding result over the threshold. Discrimination claims are therefore restricted to scheduling (D2/D3).
4. **Mutant mix.** Scheduling invalid traces are mostly arrival violations after filtering, so specificity is measured mostly on one error type.
5. **Construct validity for education.** Prompts declare ties free. Real OS courses often prescribe "smaller ID first", under which R2 would be *wrong*. Results speak to judges following stated rules, not to classroom grading.
6. **One-word verdicts.** No rationales are collected, so mechanisms (string matching vs reasoning) cannot be distinguished.
7. **Statistical power.** 630 instances, but only 5–6 models for ranking. Three-model ranking tests would be nearly uninformative.
8. **Literature search.** It was targeted and adversarial but not systematic. A closer prior study may exist.
9. **Prereg freeze is hash-only (the user's choice), not a timestamped commit.** Its strength depends on the hashes being recorded before Phase B outputs exist. They are stamped into every results file, and the values at the end of Phase A are listed below.

AGY's paper is **KILLED** as conceived: every number in it is an artifact, the "RSG exists" claim is long known, and the novelty claims are false.

A narrower paper **may** survive: a controlled measurement of valid-reference anchoring in LLM judges on oracle-checkable OS traces. Whether it does depends entirely on the preregistered rules (prereg §7), applied mechanically by `research/evaluation/analysis.py::decide`:
- **H2 and H3 both fail** → **KILL.** At most a workshop negative-result note plus the benchmark.
- **H2 holds but H2b fails** → **MODIFY** into a "context sensitivity of reference-guided judges" note.
- **H2 and H2b hold for most models, survive OOD, and do not vanish on the scheduling-only discrimination analysis** → a short paper or Findings-track submission, framed as a measurement study that extends Yeadon et al. and 2601.07506 to valid alternative references with exact oracles.
